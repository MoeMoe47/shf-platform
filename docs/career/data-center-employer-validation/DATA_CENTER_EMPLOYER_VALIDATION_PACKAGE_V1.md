## 1. Executive Overview

Silicon Heartland Foundation (SHF) operates a Career Center that helps students explore, prepare for, and connect with careers, alongside its Curriculum, Portfolio, and Credentials systems. The Data Center & AI Infrastructure pathway is one program within that Career Center, built to introduce students in grades 6 through 12 to data center operations — hardware, networking, electrical and mechanical systems, cybersecurity, and AI/cloud infrastructure — through a structured, grade-banded curriculum.

The pathway's current status under SHF's Career Pathway Acceptance Standard v1 (CPAS-1) is **PREPARE**, with a validation status of **NOT REVIEWED**. That means: SHF has built substantive curriculum, practice, and internal assessment infrastructure, but no external party — no employer, no state authority, no industry body — has yet reviewed or confirmed that this preparation reflects real, current industry practice.

SHF is requesting employer review, not because the program is unfinished in a way that blocks students from learning, but because SHF's own standard requires independent confirmation before it will describe this pathway as validated, industry-relevant, or a real route toward employment. This package exists to make that review possible, efficient, and evidence-based. SHF is not asking for a partnership commitment at this stage — only a professional review.

## 2. Pathway Status Explanation

SHF pathways move through a defined progression:

**EXPLORE → PREPARE → STATE-PATHWAY and/or EMPLOYER-VALIDATED → CAREER LAUNCH**

- **EXPLORE**: introduces a career, without implying job readiness.
- **PREPARE**: builds real foundational knowledge and skill toward a recognized next step — this is where Data Center sits today.
- **STATE-PATHWAY**: connects preparation to a verified state-recognized training/testing/licensure route (not applicable here yet — no evidence supports this classification for Data Center).
- **EMPLOYER-VALIDATED**: an external employer or industry reviewer has confirmed the curriculum and competencies are relevant to real entry-level work. **This is the review being requested in this package.**
- **CAREER LAUNCH**: the highest designation, requiring external validation plus a real, operating work-based-learning route (internship, apprenticeship, etc.).

Progression is not automatic. A pathway does not advance from PREPARE to EMPLOYER-VALIDATED because SHF believes it is ready — it advances only when real external review evidence exists. This package is how that evidence gets created, if reviewers choose to provide it.

### 2.1 Validation Decision Rule

There are four validation levels: **NOT REVIEWED → EMPLOYER REVIEWED → EMPLOYER VALIDATED → MULTIPLE-EMPLOYER VALIDATED.** Each requires more than the last, and none of them are triggered automatically:

- **NOT REVIEWED → EMPLOYER REVIEWED** requires: an identifiable organization, an identifiable reviewer, a stated reason the reviewer's role is relevant to entry-level hiring, the curriculum version reviewed, a completed Core questionnaire (Part 1), and a dated record of the feedback. Completing Part 1 of the questionnaire, with your identity, is enough to reach this level.
- **EMPLOYER REVIEWED → EMPLOYER VALIDATED** requires everything above, **plus**: an explicit validation conclusion (a specific statement, not inferred from ratings), a defined scope (which specializations or competencies), substantive engagement with Part 2 (Optional Deep Technical Review) for whatever is being validated, no unresolved critical safety or role-alignment concern, any required corrections written down, and a revalidation trigger. **A positive questionnaire alone — even a fully completed Part 1 — does not reach this level.**
- **EMPLOYER VALIDATED → MULTIPLE-EMPLOYER VALIDATED** requires independent validation, each meeting the bar above, from more than one organization.

SHF records these outcomes in a Validation / Sign-Off Record separate from your questionnaire responses — see the [Validation Record template](./DATA_CENTER_EMPLOYER_VALIDATION_RECORD_V1.json) for the exact fields.

## 7. Practical Activities

Every activity below is **SIMULATE** or **UNDERSTAND** level only — none require or permit a student to independently perform real electrical, mechanical, or security work. We use four tiers throughout this package to ask precise questions rather than vague ones: **Observe** (watch/read about a task), **Understand/Simulate** (reason through a synthetic version of the task), **Assist** (help a supervising adult with the real task), **Independently Perform** (do the real task alone). Every current Data Center activity sits at Understand/Simulate. None reach Assist or Independently Perform.

| Activity | What student does | Tier | What it attempts to demonstrate | Current evidence | Employer review question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monitoring & safe technical finding (Technical Operations) | Reviews synthetic monitoring signals (e.g. rack temperature, CPU utilization, network link status) and writes a finding distinguishing observation from conclusion, then recommends a safe next step | Understand/Simulate | Ability to interpret operational telemetry without overstating certainty | A reviewed evidence record with reviewer sign-off | Would this kind of finding be useful if a new hire produced it in their first month? |
| Connectivity troubleshooting (Networking) | Given a synthetic fault scenario (e.g. a device that can reach its gateway but not resolve a service name), identifies the most relevant fault domain | Understand/Simulate | Structured troubleshooting reasoning | Same review workflow | Does this match how you'd want a junior technician to reason through a real ticket? |
| Electrical power-path / load-capacity reasoning (Electrical) | Traces a simplified, simulated power path and reasons about load vs. stated capacity, explicitly without performing any real electrical work | Understand/Simulate only — explicitly never Assist or Independently Perform | Understanding of power distribution and capacity concepts, and recognition of the boundary between classroom reasoning and licensed electrical work | Same review workflow | Is Understand/Simulate-only the correct ceiling for this task at this age/stage, or should even that be restricted further? |
| Thermal/airflow and cooling-capacity reasoning (Mechanical/HVAC) | Interprets simulated temperature/airflow data and compares modeled cooling load to capacity | Understand/Simulate only | Environmental-systems reasoning, again without performing real HVAC work | Same review workflow | Same tier-appropriateness question as electrical, applied to HVAC. |
| Access-control and security-log analysis (Cybersecurity) | Reviews synthetic identity/permission data or security logs and applies least-privilege reasoning, without taking any real account action | Understand/Simulate | Security-reasoning fundamentals | Same review workflow | Would this kind of analysis exercise be a reasonable entry point for a security-adjacent role? |
| AI/cloud workload and capacity analysis (AI/Cloud Infrastructure) | Maps a synthetic workload's compute/memory/storage/network/power/cooling requirements, or interprets multi-node utilization signals to find a likely bottleneck | Understand/Simulate | Systems-level reasoning about AI/cloud infrastructure tradeoffs | Same review workflow | Does this reflect real entry-level exposure to AI/cloud infrastructure concerns, or is it too abstract? |
| Change/incident coordination (multiple specializations, grade 12) | Works through a simulated operational change or incident — identifying risk, following an authorized sequence, verifying outcomes, documenting rollback/escalation | Understand/Simulate | More advanced operational judgment appropriate to a graduating student | Same review workflow | Is this level of complexity appropriate for a graduating high-school student entering an entry-level role? |
| Grade-12 capstone ("Build Ohio's Next AI Data Center") | Not yet available to students — architecturally designed only | N/A — not executable | A cross-specialization team project | None yet — explicitly non-executable today | Not applicable yet; flagged here for transparency, not for review. |

All simulated/practical activities above are built on synthetic (fictional, non-production) data — none of them involve real customer systems, real electrical/mechanical work, or real security systems. See §12 for facility-access and minor-safety considerations specific to any future in-person extension of this program.

## 9. Assessment Model

- **Knowledge Assessment**: a short quiz on every one of the 168 lessons. Content-only; not independently verified by any reviewer.
- **Practical Assessment**: for 31 of 168 lessons (spanning all 6 specializations, concentrated in grades 11-12), a real, backend-recorded review process with three possible outcomes — Demonstrated, Evidence Insufficient, or Needs Review.
- **Project Assessment**: several grade-11/12 "project" lessons use the same real review workflow as Practical Assessment above; the separate Grade-12 capstone is not yet assessable because it is not yet executable.
- **Reflection**: every lesson asks a reflection question; not scored, used for metacognition only.
- **Evidence**: see §10.

**Honest gaps**: outside the 31 wired activities, no assessment result is independently verified — a student's quiz answers are not checked against a canonical answer-verification system beyond the lesson content itself, and no human reviewer sees them.

**Employer question:** Would this assessment model demonstrate meaningful readiness for your entry-level environment? What would you add or remove?

## 12. Safety, Professional Practice, and Minor/Student Considerations

### 12.1 Safety + Professional Practice

Curriculum-supported safety/professional-practice content today includes:
- **Physical and electrical safety**: explicit content distinguishing safe classroom/simulated actions from actions requiring a qualified professional (e.g., "record the evidence and escalate; do not open or touch the panel").
- **Equipment handling boundaries**: students are taught to recognize when a task requires qualified/adult intervention rather than to perform hands-on hazardous work themselves.
- **Escalation**: a recurring theme across specialization activities — "recommend a safe next step," "escalate," "document" rather than act unilaterally.
- **Documentation**: every wired activity requires the student to document findings, not just react.
- **Access/security discipline**: least-privilege reasoning and "avoid unauthorized action" framing in the cybersecurity track.
- **Teamwork/communication**: present only at the architecture level for the (currently non-executable) Grade-12 capstone; not yet a tracked competency elsewhere.

**Not yet found in curriculum**: a documented incident-response protocol for real (non-simulated) workplace scenarios; change-control content beyond the simulated "change coordination" activities; any formal PPE training.

**Employer questions:** Please validate or flag gaps in: physical safety, electrical safety, equipment handling, escalation, change control, documentation, access/security, teamwork, communication, and incident-response expectations, as they apply to a true entry-level hire at your site.

### 12.2 Minor / Student Safety and Facility Access

**Important context:** this curriculum currently runs entirely in a classroom setting on synthetic data (see §7's tier framework — every current activity is Understand/Simulate only). No student currently sets foot in a real data center facility, and no student performs Assist- or Independently-Perform-tier work of any kind as part of this program today. This section exists to get ahead of questions that would arise *if* SHF or an employer later proposed any in-person, on-site, or hands-on extension (a site visit, job shadow, or work-based-learning placement, per §16) — it is not describing something that exists today.

**SHF is not providing legal conclusions here.** The items below require employer, and where applicable legal/safety-compliance, review before any minor sets foot on a real site or touches real equipment.

- **Age restrictions**: what is your organization's minimum age policy for any facility access, and does it differ by area (e.g., office space vs. server floor vs. electrical/mechanical rooms)?
- **Facility access restrictions**: which areas of your facility, if any, could a minor visitor or student observer access, and which are categorically off-limits?
- **Security-sensitive areas**: does your facility have areas (e.g., customer cages, security operations) where any visitor access — minor or adult — is restricted regardless of supervision?
- **Hazardous equipment / electrical exposure**: what real equipment in your facility would be unsafe for an unsupervised minor to be near, even at an Observe tier?
- **Physical equipment handling**: is there any equipment a supervised minor could safely touch or operate under direct adult supervision (Assist tier), versus equipment no minor should touch under any circumstance?
- **PPE**: what personal protective equipment would be required for any level of physical access beyond a supervised office-area visit?
- **Supervision**: what supervision ratio or protocol would your organization require for a minor's visit or work-based-learning placement?
- **Background/security policies**: does your organization require background checks, escorts, or other clearance for any visitor, and would that differ for a minor?
- **Tasks minors cannot or should not perform**: independent of what SHF's curriculum teaches in the classroom, what real tasks in your facility should never be assigned to a minor, even under supervision?
- **Observe vs. Simulate vs. Assist vs. Independently Perform**: for any future in-person exposure, which of these four tiers (defined in §7) would your organization be comfortable extending to a minor student, and under what conditions?

These questions are intentionally more detailed than the rest of this package because minor safety is not an area where SHF is willing to guess — any future in-person work-based-learning discussion (§16) must be grounded in your organization's actual policies, not SHF's assumptions.

## 18. Review / Renewal Schedule

This package and any resulting validation are tied to a specific curriculum version and are not intended to be permanent:

- **Validation date**: recorded at the time of sign-off (see the Validation Record).
- **Curriculum version reviewed**: recorded at the time of sign-off — curriculum content changes over time and a prior review does not automatically cover later changes.
- **Reviewer**: named individual and organization, recorded at sign-off.
- **Scope**: exactly which specializations/roles/activities were reviewed — a review of one specialization does not validate the others.
- **Expiration / next review date**: to be set by the reviewer and SHF jointly at sign-off; SHF recommends no more than 24 months without re-review, and sooner if curriculum changes materially.
- **Material-change trigger**: if SHF materially changes the curriculum, competencies, or safety content for a reviewed specialization, that specialization's validation should be treated as lapsed until re-reviewed.
- **Re-validation requirements**: same process as the original review, scoped to what changed.
