# Data Center & AI Infrastructure — Outreach Brief v1

A short reference document — suitable as an email attachment, leave-behind, or talking-points sheet for an introductory conversation. Not a replacement for the full [Employer Validation Package](../DATA_CENTER_EMPLOYER_VALIDATION_PACKAGE_V1.md).

## Program Overview

Silicon Heartland Foundation (SHF) runs a Data Center & AI Infrastructure curriculum for grades 6-12, covering data center systems, hardware and networking fundamentals, electrical and mechanical (HVAC) concepts, cybersecurity fundamentals, and AI/cloud infrastructure — through 168 lessons across 7 grade bands, including 6 specialization tracks at grades 11-12.

## Target Learner

Students in grades 6 through 12, progressing from general career discovery (grades 6-8) through exploration (grades 9-10) into specialized preparation (grades 11-12).

## Target Role(s)

**Primary:** Data Center Technician (SHF's canonical target occupation for this pathway).
**Related/exploratory:** Network, Electrical, HVAC/Mechanical, Cybersecurity, and Cloud/AI Infrastructure Technician — curriculum-supported but not yet formally defined as SHF career records.

## Current Status

**Pathway Status: PREPARE.** **Validation Status: NOT REVIEWED.** No employer or industry body has reviewed this program yet.

## Review Goal

Determine whether this curriculum, as it exists today, reflects real entry-level work — and if not, what would need to change.

## What SHF Wants Feedback On

- Whether the target role(s) are correctly identified
- Whether curriculum scope and grade-level progression are appropriate
- Whether the technical competencies and practical activities are realistic
- Whether the assessment and evidence approach would be meaningful in a hiring context
- Which credentials, if any, actually matter for entry-level hiring
- Safety expectations — including, given the student age range, facility-access and minor-safety boundaries
- Whether any future work-based-learning route would be feasible for the reviewer's organization

## What SHF Is NOT Asking For

A hiring commitment, an internship or apprenticeship agreement, a credential endorsement, a partnership of any kind, or a guarantee of any future action. Participation is voluntary and carries no obligation.

## Package Contents

The full package includes: program overview and status, target roles, curriculum and competency summary, practical activities, learning journey, assessment and evidence model, credential strategy, safety and minor/student-safety considerations, accessibility notes, and (clearly marked as not-yet-built) Learning Arcade and Metaverse simulation concepts. It comes with a two-part questionnaire — a Core section and an Optional Deep Technical section — and SHF's own internal record-keeping templates (not for the reviewer to complete).

## Review Outcome Options

Completing the Core questionnaire, with your identity and organization, is enough for SHF to record this as **Employer Reviewed**. Reaching **Employer Validated** requires more: an explicit conclusion from you, a defined scope, deeper technical engagement where relevant, and no unresolved safety or role-alignment concern. A positive impression alone does not produce a validated outcome — SHF's standard is explicit about this so reviewers aren't surprised by what their time does and doesn't accomplish.

## Next Steps After Review

SHF records the feedback internally, determines whether it supports Employer Reviewed or Employer Validated (and for what scope), documents any required corrections, and shares back what changed as a result. Any interest in future work-based learning, advisory involvement, or other partnership is discussed separately, only if and when the reviewer chooses to raise it.
