# Data Center & AI Infrastructure — First Employer Validation Cohort Plan v1

This plan defines **target reviewer types**, not actual organizations or individuals. No company is named unless the repository already documents an existing SHF relationship — none was found for Data Center specifically during this or prior phases, so none is named here. Identifying real candidates is a separate, later step (see §Next Steps in the Phase 3 report).

## Why Three Perspectives

No single reviewer can credibly assess this whole program. A data center operator can speak to whether the target role and day-to-day work are realistic; an infrastructure/facilities/technical employer can speak to the physical, safety, and technical-skill dimensions the operator role touches only partially; and a workforce/education/training partner can speak to whether SHF's preparation actually connects to something real beyond SHF's own classroom — a credential, a training provider, or a work-based-learning pipeline. Reviewing with only one of these perspectives would leave the other two domains unvalidated no matter how positive that one review was.

## A. Data Center Operator

**Ideal reviewer role:** Data Center Operations Manager, Data Center Technician Lead, or a technical hiring manager who screens or supervises entry-level data center hires.

**Why this perspective matters:** This reviewer sees actual entry-level data center technicians on the job. They are the most direct source of truth for whether SHF's target role (Data Center Technician) and its associated competencies reflect real day-one work, not an idealized or outdated version of it.

**Package sections to prioritize:** §3 (Target Roles), §6 (Competency Map), §7 (Practical Activities), §9 (Assessment Model), §11 (Credential Strategy), Questionnaire Part 1 in full.

**Is the Optional Deep Technical section especially useful for this reviewer?** Yes, particularly sections P (Monitoring & Troubleshooting) and Q (Incident/Escalation Practices) in the questionnaire — these map directly to daily operator-level work.

**What evidence SHF needs from this reviewer:** A specific answer on whether "Data Center Technician" is the right primary target role, a rating on whether the 41 wired practical activities reflect real entry-level tasks, and — critically — an answer to Questionnaire K4 (any critical safety or role-alignment concern).

**What validation outcome could reasonably result:** At minimum, **Employer Reviewed** from a completed Core questionnaire. If this reviewer also engages with the Deep Technical monitoring/troubleshooting sections and provides an explicit, scoped conclusion, **Employer Validated for the Technical Operations specialization** is realistic — but only for that specialization, not the other five, unless the reviewer's expertise and stated scope actually cover them.

## B. Infrastructure / Facilities / Technical Employer or Contractor

**Ideal reviewer role:** Facilities Manager, Critical Facilities Engineer, Network Infrastructure Manager, or Infrastructure Operations Manager — someone responsible for the physical plant (power, cooling, networking, physical security) rather than day-to-day IT operations alone.

**Why this perspective matters:** This program's electrical, mechanical/HVAC, and networking specializations describe physical-infrastructure concepts SHF explicitly does not let students touch in real life (simulation/reasoning only). This reviewer is the right person to judge whether that simulated content is technically accurate and whether the safety boundary SHF has drawn is the correct one — including, importantly, the minor/student safety and facility-access questions this program's age range raises.

**Package sections to prioritize:** §7 (Practical Activities, especially the electrical/HVAC/networking rows), §12 (Safety, Professional Practice, and Minor/Student Considerations, both 12.1 and 12.2), §13 (Accessibility + Learner Support, for physical-access considerations), Questionnaire Part 1 Sections E (Safety) and D, plus Part 2 Sections L-S (Physical Infrastructure through Facility Access/Security).

**Is the Optional Deep Technical section especially useful for this reviewer?** Yes — this is the reviewer type Part 2 was built for. Sections L (Physical Infrastructure), N (Power Systems), O (Cooling Systems), and S (Facility Access/Security) are most relevant.

**What evidence SHF needs from this reviewer:** Concrete answers to the minor/student safety questions (Questionnaire E3-E8) — age restrictions, restricted areas, hazardous equipment, PPE, and which of the four exposure tiers (Observe/Understand-Simulate/Assist/Independently Perform) their organization would ever extend to a minor. This evidence is foundational for any future work-based-learning discussion (see §Work-Based Learning below), independent of whether this reviewer validates anything else.

**What validation outcome could reasonably result:** **Employer Reviewed** at minimum. Given deep technical engagement, **Employer Validated for Electrical Infrastructure, Mechanical/HVAC, and/or Networking-Fiber specializations** specifically — scoped narrowly to what this reviewer's expertise actually covers.

## C. Workforce / Education / Training Partner

**Ideal reviewer role:** Workforce Development Manager, Apprenticeship Coordinator, Technical Program Director, or Community College Faculty/Program Lead in a relevant technical field.

**Why this perspective matters:** This reviewer can assess something neither of the other two can: whether SHF's PREPARE-stage curriculum actually connects to something recognized beyond SHF — a real training pathway, a credential worth pursuing, or an apprenticeship/work-based-learning pipeline. This is the perspective most relevant to eventually reaching STATE-PATHWAY or a credible work-based-learning discussion, even though this phase does not build either.

**Package sections to prioritize:** §2/§2.1 (Pathway Status and Validation Decision Rule), §5 (Curriculum Summary), §11 (Credential Strategy), §16 (Proposed Work-Based-Learning Model), §17 (Partnership Options), Questionnaire Part 1 Sections C, I, and J.

**Is the Optional Deep Technical section especially useful for this reviewer?** Less than for A or B — this reviewer's value is mostly in Part 1 (curriculum-to-pathway alignment, credentials, work-based learning), though Section U (Practical Realism/Role-Specific Skill Gaps) may still be useful if they have direct program-design experience.

**What evidence SHF needs from this reviewer:** A specific answer on which third-party credentials are actually worth pursuing (Questionnaire I2/I3), and candid feedback on whether SHF's PREPARE-stage content is a credible foundation for a real training or apprenticeship pipeline, or whether more is needed first.

**What validation outcome could reasonably result:** **Employer Reviewed** is the realistic baseline. This reviewer type is less likely than A or B to produce a scoped **Employer Validated** on technical competencies specifically (that's not their expertise), but their feedback is the most likely to shape whether SHF later pursues STATE-PATHWAY work or a formal training partnership — a separate, future decision, not something this phase makes.

## Outreach Sequence

1. **Initial email/introduction** — the outreach email, asking only whether the recipient is open to reviewing, before sending the full package.
2. **Send the employer validation package** — once a reviewer confirms interest, send the cover letter, the full package, and the questionnaire.
3. **Reviewer completes the Core questionnaire (Part 1)** — sufficient on its own for an Employer Reviewed record.
4. **Optional Deep Technical review (Part 2)** — only for reviewers with the relevant expertise and time; not required.
5. **SHF records feedback** — in the Employer Feedback Record, attributed to the named reviewer and organization.
6. **SHF determines Employer Reviewed vs. sufficiency for Employer Validated** — using the sufficiency requirements in the Validation Record and package §2.1. This is an SHF decision, made against the recorded evidence, not something the reviewer's ratings alone trigger.
7. **Record required corrections** — whatever the reviewer flagged as needing to change, recorded in the Validation Record's `required_corrections` field regardless of the overall outcome.
8. **If appropriate, discuss future partnership opportunities separately** — only after the review process is complete, only if the reviewer expresses interest (§17 Partnership Options), and always as a distinct conversation from the validation outcome itself.

## Follow-Up Principles

- No pressure language, ever. Do not say or imply "we only need your signature," "this should be quick," or anything suggesting the outcome is a formality.
- No assumption of approval. A non-response, a partial response, or a critical response are all legitimate outcomes SHF should plan for, not obstacles to route around.
- Preferred framing for any follow-up: *"We are seeking substantive feedback, including any areas you believe should be corrected, removed, or strengthened."*
- If a reviewer raises a critical safety or role-alignment concern (Questionnaire K4), that concern must be recorded and addressed before any validated outcome is considered for the affected scope — it is not something to negotiate past.

## Validation Outcome Governance (reinforced for outreach)

Every piece of outreach material in this folder states, in some form: participation does not guarantee validation; feedback may result in required changes rather than validation; positive feedback alone is not automatically validation; SHF controls the pathway-status record (a reviewer's opinion informs it, but does not directly edit it); and any validated outcome must state an explicit, bounded scope. The full progression — NOT REVIEWED → EMPLOYER REVIEWED → EMPLOYER VALIDATED → MULTIPLE-EMPLOYER VALIDATED — and its sufficiency requirements live in `docs/career/data-center-employer-validation/DATA_CENTER_EMPLOYER_VALIDATION_RECORD_V1.json` and package §2.1; outreach material should point to, not restate or loosen, that rule.

## Work-Based Learning, Framed Correctly

Outreach material may mention that SHF is also interested in future feedback about site visits, job shadowing, mentorship, employer projects, internships, pre-apprenticeship, apprenticeship exploration, and interview pathways — but always as **optional future discussion**, explicitly separate from the validation request itself. No outreach material in this folder asks a reviewer to commit to any of these as part of completing a review.

## Minor / Student Safety in Outreach

Because this program serves grades 6-12, every outreach document in this folder explicitly signals that SHF especially values feedback on age restrictions, facility access, safety, supervision, and which tasks are appropriate for observation, simulation, supervised practice, or are inappropriate for minors under any circumstance. None of these materials offer a legal conclusion on any of these points — they consistently ask the reviewer to state their own organization's policy.
