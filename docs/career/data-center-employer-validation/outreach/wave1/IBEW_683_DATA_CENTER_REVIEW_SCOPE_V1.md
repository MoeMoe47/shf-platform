# IBEW Local 683 / Electrical Trades Center — Review Scope Document v1

One-page reference for the Electrical Trades Center reviewer. Reviewer type: **APPRENTICESHIP / WORKFORCE** — explicitly *not* treated as SHF's sole employer validator for this pathway (see Governance Distinction below).

## Why You

Your organization has real, direct, current involvement in Central Ohio's data center construction boom, and runs a real, substantial Registered Apprenticeship with an existing precedent for transitioning high school students into apprenticeship standing. That combination of hands-on technical experience and direct exposure to the student-to-apprentice transition question makes your review uniquely valuable for our electrical-infrastructure content specifically.

## Governance Distinction (important)

The Electrical Trades Center is not being treated as the sole or primary employer validator for the Data Center pathway. Your review is one of three complementary perspectives SHF is seeking in this cohort (employer/operator, apprenticeship/workforce, and training/education). A favorable review from you supports "Employer Reviewed" status and informs our electrical-infrastructure and safety content specifically — it does not, by itself, constitute the pathway's "Employer Validated" status, which requires input from a hiring employer/operator reviewing workplace relevance directly.

## What We Would Value Your Review Of

- Electrical fundamentals as taught in our curriculum
- Physical infrastructure concepts (power paths, load/capacity reasoning)
- Safety boundaries — specifically whether "reasoning/simulation only, never real electrical work" is the correct ceiling for a K-12 program
- PPE and supervision expectations for any future in-person exposure
- Age-appropriateness of our content and sequencing
- What a real transition from a K-12 program into apprenticeship-level readiness should look like
- Worksite expectations a student should understand before any real exposure
- The distinction between observation, simulation, supervised practice, and independent work as it applies to electrical tasks specifically
- Which skills genuinely require formal apprenticeship/training rather than classroom preparation

## What We Are NOT Asking You to Validate

That SHF students would be admitted into, or sponsored for, an IBEW apprenticeship. We are not asking about apprenticeship capacity, application processes, or any commitment — those would be a separate future conversation, not part of this review.

## Core Questionnaire Sections to Prioritize

E (Safety, including Minor/Student Safety E3-E8 — this is where your perspective is most valuable), F (Professional Practice, including teamwork), J (Work-Based Learning, framed as future/exploratory only).

## Optional Deep Technical Sections

**Recommended for the applicable electrical/safety sections only**: N (Power Systems), L (Physical Infrastructure), S (Facility Access/Security). Sections outside electrical/power/safety (e.g., cooling systems, cybersecurity) are not expected from this reviewer.

## Student / Minor Safety Questions Relevant to You

This is the area where your input matters most: real electrical hazards, what a minor could ever safely observe near real (non-simulated) electrical equipment, PPE requirements, supervision ratios your training center would expect, and — given your organization's real student-partner precedent — what a responsible, staged path from classroom reasoning to supervised practice to eventual apprenticeship-level work should look like. We are not asking for a legal conclusion, only your organization's real standards and judgment.
