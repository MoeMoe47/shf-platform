## 4. SHF formation-readiness checklist

### A. Information to collect securely

- [ ] Exact proposed name: **Silicon Heartland Foundation**
- [ ] Principal office location in Ohio
- [ ] Professional statutory agent's accepted legal name and Ohio street address
- [ ] Incorporator identity and signing authority
- [ ] Proposed effective date, if not immediate
- [ ] Initial director candidates and independence/conflict information
- [ ] Proposed officer roles and signing limits
- [ ] Secure custody location for accepted filings, board records, EIN confirmation, tax filings, and privileged advice

Do not place private addresses, signatures, EINs, background-check results, or privileged material in source control.

### B. Counsel review before filing

- [ ] Confirm the entity type and Ohio formation approach.
- [ ] Review the charitable and educational purpose statement against the complete SHF mission and planned activities.
- [ ] Include appropriate limitations on private benefit, political campaign activity, lobbying, and non-charitable activity.
- [ ] Include an appropriate dissolution/asset-dedication provision.
- [ ] Confirm whether any additional provisions belong in the Articles rather than bylaws or policies.
- [ ] Review the SHF–SHS founder relationship and planned related-party controls before documents are adopted.

Ohio's official guide says a domestic nonprofit is legally organized by filing Form 532B. The IRS explains that a full Form 1023 application requires an exact copy of the organizing document and that the organizing document must contain qualifying provisions. Review the [Ohio nonprofit guide](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/business-start-a-nonprofit.pdf), [Ohio Form 532B](https://www.ohiosos.gov/assets/532b-web.pdf), and [IRS sample public-charity organizing provisions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/sample-organizing-documents-public-charity).

### C. Governance package to prepare for adoption after formation

- [ ] Bylaws defining board authority, terms, meetings, quorum, voting, officers, committees, records, amendments, removal, vacancies, and dissolution governance
- [ ] Conflict-of-interest policy
- [ ] Related-party transaction review policy
- [ ] Independent approval and recusal procedure
- [ ] Whistleblower and records-management policies appropriate to organizational size and risk
- [ ] Financial authority and segregation-of-duties policy
- [ ] Restricted-fund and grant-compliance policy
- [ ] Data/privacy, youth safety, accessibility, incident response, and complaints policies
- [ ] Initial organizational resolutions and officer appointments
- [ ] Signing-authority schedule
- [ ] Annual disclosure and governance-review calendar

The IRS describes a conflict policy as a process for disclosure and recusal when a director's duty to the charity conflicts with financial interests, including a contract involving a director-owned business. See the [IRS conflict-of-interest explanation](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/form-1023-purpose-of-conflict-of-interest-policy).

### D. Board recruitment gate

Target: at least three independent directors whose combined experience covers nonprofit governance, education/workforce, finance, legal/compliance, technology, and community needs.

For each candidate, securely document:

- [ ] qualifications and mission connection;
- [ ] independence from SHS and other financial interests;
- [ ] family, business, compensation, vendor, and funding relationships;
- [ ] willingness to fulfill fiduciary and oversight responsibilities;
- [ ] availability for meetings, training, and annual disclosures;
- [ ] required screening appropriate to access and youth-facing responsibilities.

Candidate selection and legal independence criteria require counsel review. The [Ohio Attorney General's board-member guide](https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/guideforcharityboardmembers) is an official orientation source.

### E. EIN and federal exemption gate

- [ ] File reviewed Ohio Articles and obtain accepted formation evidence.
- [ ] Apply directly to the IRS for SHF's EIN; retain the confirmation securely.
- [ ] Do not expose the EIN in Git or public materials.
- [ ] Determine with counsel whether Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ is appropriate; do not assume eligibility for the streamlined form.
- [ ] Prepare the complete narrative, activities, compensation, related-party, financial, governance, fundraising, grantmaking, scholarship, school, youth, international, IP, technology, and data-processing disclosures applicable to SHF.
- [ ] Obtain authorization from the properly constituted organization before submission.
- [ ] Submit through Pay.gov only after review and retain the filed copy and evidence securely.

The IRS says an organization should be legally formed before applying for an EIN, an EIN is required for Form 1023, and Form 1023-series applications are submitted electronically through Pay.gov. See the [IRS charities and nonprofits page](https://www.irs.gov/charities-and-nonprofits), [Form 1023 FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-1023), and [application page](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/application-for-recognition-of-exemption).

### F. Ohio charity and tax follow-up

- [ ] Determine Ohio Attorney General charity-registration timing and any exemption with counsel.
- [ ] Establish annual state and federal filing calendar.
- [ ] Determine applicable Ohio tax registrations and exemptions with the accountant.
- [ ] Determine fundraising, charitable solicitation, gaming, scholarship, employment, and local requirements before those activities begin.

The Ohio Attorney General states that most Ohio charities must register and file annual reports, subject to exceptions. See [Ohio charitable registration](https://charitableregistration.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/) and the [official information sheet](https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Files/Publications-Files/Publications-for-Business/Online-Charitable-Registration-Information-Sheet).

## 6. SHF–SHS relationship readiness

No shared service, license, data flow, staffing arrangement, grant support, sponsorship, public claim, or cost allocation should be treated as authorized merely because both organizations share a founder or platform.

Before shared operations, prepare for independent review:

- [ ] Master relationship agreement
- [ ] Technology and IP license
- [ ] Shared services and cost-allocation agreement
- [ ] Data-processing/data-sharing agreement
- [ ] Related-party disclosure package
- [ ] Comparable cost or fair-market support
- [ ] SHF independent review, recusal, minutes, and approval
- [ ] Separate invoices, books, bank accounts, and asset records
- [ ] Data ownership, access, purpose, retention, export, deletion, and incident allocation
- [ ] Termination, transition assistance, portability, credential revocation, and capability shutdown
- [ ] Annual legal, accounting, insurance, and board review

The IRS states that a 501(c)(3) organization must not be organized or operated for private interests and describes consequences for excess-benefit transactions. See the [IRS 501(c)(3) exemption requirements](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/exemption-requirements-501c3-organizations).
