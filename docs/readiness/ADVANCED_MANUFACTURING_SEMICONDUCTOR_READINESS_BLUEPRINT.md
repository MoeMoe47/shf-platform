## 1. Course Purpose

Prepare learners to understand advanced manufacturing and semiconductor workplaces, develop production-ready professional habits, recognize safety and quality expectations, understand automation and technical systems at a readiness level, and make informed next-step career decisions.

This course is readiness education.

It does not independently qualify a learner to operate industrial machinery, perform regulated technical work, enter controlled production environments, or claim an industry credential.

## 2. Intended Audience

Primary audiences may include:

- secondary students
- youth workforce participants
- adult career changers
- pre-apprenticeship learners
- learners exploring CNC and machining
- learners exploring industrial maintenance
- learners exploring robotics and automation
- learners exploring semiconductor manufacturing
- learners preparing for employer or partner technical training

## 3. Architectural Position

Universal Career Readiness
→ Advanced Manufacturing & Semiconductor Readiness
→ Career / occupation-specific preparation
→ Employer / partner technical training
→ Industry credential or apprenticeship where applicable
→ Employment

This course owns the preparation/readiness layer only.

## 9. Initial Proof Activity Candidates

### Manufacturing Quality Check

Learner reviews a simulated production scenario and identifies:

- specification
- observed condition
- possible defect
- verification step
- escalation path

Potential measures:
- attention to detail
- verification
- documentation
- appropriate escalation

### Safety and Stop-Work Decision

Learner analyzes a workplace scenario and identifies when work should stop and who should be notified.

Potential measures:
- hazard recognition
- scope awareness
- communication
- escalation

### Production Troubleshooting Exercise

Learner separates symptoms, facts, assumptions, and next verification steps.

Potential measures:
- structured problem solving
- systems thinking
- documentation
- no unsafe intervention

### Semiconductor Contamination Scenario

Learner identifies behaviors that could threaten a controlled environment and explains the appropriate response.

Potential measures:
- contamination awareness
- process discipline
- verification
- escalation

These remain candidates until competency and proof configuration audits are complete.

## 14. Safety Boundary

The course must distinguish between:

Awareness
→ Readiness
→ Supervised technical training
→ Employer / partner authorization
→ Independent role performance

SHU readiness content must not represent the learner as authorized to:

- operate machinery unsupervised
- perform electrical maintenance
- bypass guards or interlocks
- conduct lockout/tagout independently
- handle hazardous production chemicals
- enter restricted fab areas
- perform regulated semiconductor processes

## 15. Completion Boundary

- CNC certification
