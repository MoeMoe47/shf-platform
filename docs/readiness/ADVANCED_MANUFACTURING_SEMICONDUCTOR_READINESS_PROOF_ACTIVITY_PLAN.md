→ Evidence
→ Authorized Review
→ Competency Decision

## 3. Proof Density Principle

The proposed course contains 75 lessons.

It should not contain 75 competency proofs.

Most lessons are intended to:

- orient
- teach
- contextualize
- practice
- assess understanding
- produce evidence candidates
- support reflection

Formal proof should be limited to a small number of synthesis activities that demonstrate meaningful readiness capability.

## 7. Safety / Scope — Target Capability

The learner should demonstrate the ability to:

- recognize a potential manufacturing hazard
- distinguish facts from assumptions
- identify the limits of their authorization
- stop when conditions are unsafe or unclear
- avoid improvising outside scope
- identify an appropriate escalation path
- communicate the concern accurately
- preserve uncertainty
- avoid representing readiness as qualification

This is workplace safety and scope awareness.

It is not technical authorization.

## 9. Safety / Scope — Proposed Scenario Shape

The learner receives a synthetic manufacturing workplace scenario containing:

- assigned learner role
- task request
- production context
- visible or reported condition
- stated authorization boundary

Scenario categories may include:

- unfamiliar equipment condition
- guarding concern
- electrical concern
- thermal condition
- material-handling concern
- contamination concern
- abnormal automated-system state
- unclear procedure
- request outside training
- quality concern with safety implications

## 12. Safety / Scope — Safety Boundary

The proof must not require learners to:

- operate industrial machinery
- open electrical panels
- perform live electrical measurements
- bypass guarding
- defeat interlocks
- perform lockout/tagout as a qualified worker
- repair machinery
- modify PLC logic
- program a production robot
- handle hazardous chemicals
- enter a cleanroom as an authorized worker
- perform semiconductor production tasks

All scenarios must remain synthetic, simulated, classroom-safe, or otherwise explicitly governed.

## 13. Safety / Scope — Pass / Revise Intent

Potential successful performance should show the learner:

- recognizes the concern
- avoids unsafe action
- identifies what is known
- identifies what is uncertain
- stays within scope
- chooses a reasonable escalation path
- communicates accurately

Potential revise conditions include:

- unsafe action selected
- authorization boundary ignored
- unsupported assumptions treated as facts
- escalation omitted
- technical authority overstated

Canonical criteria remain unresolved.

## 14. Safety / Scope — Accessibility

The scenario must not depend solely on:

- color
- sound
- fine motor interaction
- inaccessible diagrams
- timed physical manipulation

Equivalent accessible representations must preserve the same reasoning challenge.

## 17. Problem-Solving / Quality — Target Capability

The learner should demonstrate the ability to:

- define a manufacturing problem
- distinguish facts from assumptions
- use supplied evidence
- identify relevant requirements
- compare possible causes
- identify missing information
- compare response options
- recognize when quality or process concerns require escalation
- communicate a bounded recommendation
- document uncertainty

This is readiness-level technical reasoning.

It is not engineering authority.

It is not product-release authority.

## 22. Problem-Solving / Quality — Quality Boundary

The proof may ask whether supplied synthetic output appears to meet a supplied synthetic requirement.

It must not authorize the learner to:

- release product
- disposition nonconforming product
- approve deviation
- approve engineering change
- sign quality records as an authorized inspector
- establish acceptance criteria
- override process controls

## 23. Problem-Solving / Quality — Measurement Boundary

If measurements are used:

- values must be supplied or safely simulated
- units and tolerances must be explicit
- no live industrial measurement is required
- no calibration authority is implied
- no inspection qualification is implied

## 24. Problem-Solving / Quality — Pass / Revise Intent

Potential successful performance should show:

- problem is defined clearly
- supplied evidence is used accurately
- assumptions are labeled
- requirement comparison is correct
- uncertainty remains visible
- recommendation remains within scope
- escalation occurs when authorization is required

Potential revise conditions include:

- unsupported root-cause claim
- incorrect requirement interpretation
- ignored uncertainty
- unauthorized production action
- unsupported product-release decision

## 25. Problem-Solving / Quality — Accessibility

Technical tables, measurements, diagrams and process-flow information must have accessible equivalents.

Evaluation should focus on reasoning rather than visual format alone.
