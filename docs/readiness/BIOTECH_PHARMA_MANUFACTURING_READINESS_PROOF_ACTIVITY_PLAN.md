## 10. Proof Candidate 1 — Controlled Environment / Cleanroom Entry Scenario

### Purpose

Evaluate whether a learner can review a simulated controlled-environment entry situation, identify relevant readiness-level risks and boundaries, recognize when entry or action requires qualified authorization, document concerns, and choose a safe escalation path.

### Proposed Classification

NEW DOMAIN ACTIVITY

POSSIBLE TRANSFERABLE COMPETENCY REUSE

No new cleanroom competency is assumed.

## 11. Proof Candidate 1 — Why It Matters

Controlled environments are central to:

- biotechnology manufacturing
- pharmaceutical manufacturing
- contamination awareness
- process discipline
- personnel behavior
- quality culture

The repository does not currently prove a canonical biotechnology cleanroom proof.

## 12. Proof Candidate 1 — Scenario Shape

The learner may receive a synthetic packet containing:

- fictional facility
- simplified controlled-area map
- fictional access zone
- simulated entry instructions
- mock PPE / gowning requirements
- synthetic training status
- fictional authorization status
- simulated contamination concern
- mock material restriction
- synthetic environmental status
- stated escalation procedure

No real cleanroom is required.

## 13. Proof Candidate 1 — Required Learner Actions

The learner should:

1. identify the controlled area
2. identify stated entry requirements
3. identify missing authorization
4. identify contamination concerns
5. identify relevant PPE or behavior expectations
6. distinguish awareness from qualification
7. identify actions outside learner scope
8. document what is known
9. document what is missing
10. choose a safe next step
11. identify the qualified escalation path
12. avoid claiming authorization

## 14. Proof Candidate 1 — Safe Scope

Permitted:

- diagrams
- synthetic entry records
- fictional training status
- static PPE examples
- mock labels
- simulated environmental information
- documentation
- reasoning
- escalation

## 35. Proof Candidate 3 — Quality Deviation Scenario

### Purpose

Evaluate whether a learner can analyze a synthetic manufacturing deviation, separate facts from assumptions, identify affected records or process steps, preserve uncertainty, document the issue, and escalate to appropriate qualified quality authority.

### Proposed Classification

NEW DOMAIN ACTIVITY

POSSIBLE TRANSFERABLE PROBLEM-SOLVING REUSE

## 36. Proof Candidate 3 — Why It Matters

Deviation reasoning integrates:

- quality culture
- documentation
- data integrity
- evidence
- process awareness
- controlled change
- scope
- escalation
- verification

It is a strong capstone-style readiness activity without requiring regulated authority.

## 37. Proof Candidate 3 — Scenario Shape

A synthetic deviation packet may contain:

- fictional process step
- expected instruction
- observed event
- timestamp
- mock operator note
- synthetic equipment status
- synthetic material status
- relevant batch-record excerpt
- prior approved instruction
- one missing fact
- one conflicting detail

## 38. Proof Candidate 3 — Required Learner Actions

The learner should:

1. state the expected condition
2. state the observed condition
3. identify the difference
4. identify supplied evidence
5. separate fact from assumption
6. identify missing information
7. identify affected documentation
8. identify relevant traceability
9. identify possible downstream concern
10. preserve uncertainty
11. document the deviation clearly
12. identify the authorized escalation path
13. avoid root-cause overclaiming
14. avoid disposition decisions

## 39. Proof Candidate 3 — Root Cause Boundary
