## 7. Primary Proof 1

Title:

Business Transaction Workflow Review

Purpose:

Evaluate whether a learner can interpret a synthetic business transaction packet and reason through invoice, AP, AR, documentation, approval, matching, exception, and escalation concepts without executing a real financial transaction.

## 8. Proof 1 Scope

The scenario may include synthetic:

- vendor invoice
- customer invoice
- purchase order
- receiving record
- receipt
- approval record
- payment-status record
- due date
- customer terms
- exception flag
- supporting documentation

## 9. Proof 1 Required Learner Actions

The learner should:

1. identify the purpose of each record
2. distinguish AP from AR
3. identify relevant parties
4. trace the transaction sequence
5. identify missing or conflicting information
6. identify whether an approval is present
7. identify whether supporting records align
8. distinguish documentation from payment authority
9. recommend an appropriate next step
10. escalate unresolved exceptions

## 10. Proof 1 Observable Criteria

Candidate criteria:

- correctly distinguishes payable and receivable workflow
- identifies relevant source documents
- traces the transaction sequence accurately
- identifies at least one discrepancy or exception when present
- distinguishes evidence from assumption
- identifies approval and authorization boundaries
- does not claim payment authority
- recommends a documented next step
- escalates unresolved issues appropriately
- preserves confidentiality and synthetic-data boundaries

## 11. Proof 1 Non-Goals

This proof does not establish:

- bookkeeping certification
- accounting qualification
- payment authority
- bank access
- accounts-payable job qualification
- accounts-receivable job qualification
- tax authority
- procurement authority

## 14. Primary Proof 2

Title:

Reconciliation & Internal Controls Analysis

Purpose:

Evaluate whether a learner can compare synthetic records, identify discrepancies, assess supporting evidence, recognize internal-control issues, and recommend a safe escalation path.

## 15. Proof 2 Scope

The scenario may include:

- synthetic transaction register
- fictional bank-statement extract
- fictional invoice list
- fictional receipts
- fictional purchase records
- fictional payment-status records
- approval records
- timestamps
- duplicate transaction
- missing documentation
- mismatched amount
- unauthorized change
- separation-of-duties concern

## 16. Proof 2 Required Learner Actions

The learner should:

1. compare two or more synthetic record sets
2. identify matching entries
3. identify discrepancies
4. classify discrepancy type
5. identify supporting evidence
6. identify missing evidence
7. distinguish error from confirmed fraud
8. identify internal-control weakness
9. identify separation-of-duties concerns
10. recommend an authorized next step

## 17. Proof 2 Observable Criteria

Candidate criteria:

- compares records systematically
- identifies material discrepancies accurately
- uses relevant supporting evidence
- preserves uncertainty
- distinguishes discrepancy from confirmed fraud
- identifies control weakness where present
- recognizes separation-of-duties issues
- documents the audit trail
- recommends appropriate escalation
- avoids unauthorized correction or transaction execution

## 18. Proof 2 Non-Goals

This proof does not establish:

- auditor qualification
- accountant qualification
- fraud-investigator authority
- bank-reconciliation authority
- general-ledger authority
- regulatory authority
- payment authority

## 21. Primary Proof 3

Title:

Business Operations Decision & Escalation Scenario

Purpose:

Evaluate whether a learner can analyze a cross-functional synthetic business situation, identify authority boundaries, compare reasonable options, and document an appropriate operational decision and escalation.

## 22. Proof 3 Scope

The scenario may combine:

- purchasing
- procurement
- payroll
- customer service
- Sales handoff
- payment status
- cash-flow constraints
- vendor issue
- customer issue
- confidentiality
- deadline
- authorization
- operational risk

## 23. Proof 3 Required Learner Actions

The learner should:

1. define the operational problem
2. separate facts from assumptions
3. identify missing information
4. identify affected roles
5. identify authority boundaries
6. compare reasonable options
7. evaluate operational tradeoffs
8. protect confidential information
9. choose a safe authorized next step
10. document escalation and follow-up

## 24. Proof 3 Observable Criteria

Candidate criteria:

- defines the business problem using relevant facts
- distinguishes facts, assumptions, and missing information
- identifies affected stakeholders and roles
- recognizes approval and authority boundaries
- compares options using appropriate criteria
- considers customer, cost, time, ethics, and operational impact
- preserves financial-data safety
- recommends an authorized next step
- identifies escalation when required
- documents follow-up clearly
