## 2. Course Intent

Construction & Skilled Trades Readiness prepares learners to understand construction workplaces, jobsite culture, trade families, safety, plans and specifications, tools and materials, measurement, quality, sequencing, electrical and HVAC awareness, plumbing awareness, building systems, teamwork, career pathways, evidence, and transition to further technical training or apprenticeship.

The course is a readiness layer.

It is not a substitute for:

- registered apprenticeship
- employer-specific qualification
- electrician licensure
- HVAC licensure or certification
- plumbing licensure
- OSHA-authorized training where separately required
- equipment operator qualification
- trade-union membership
- journeyperson status
- contractor licensing

## 3. Approved Structural Direction

Pass 2 preserves that structure.

## 4. Classification Model

Each lesson is classified as:

### TRANSFERABLE

The core learner capability is cross-industry and should preferentially reuse Universal Career Readiness.

### CONTEXTUALIZED

The capability already exists but requires construction-specific examples, workflows, risks, or terminology.

### INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC

The objective is genuinely construction-specific and cannot be satisfied by changing examples alone.

Industry-specific does not mean licensed, regulated, or employer-authorized.

## 5. Curriculum Composition Principle

Preferred composition:

Universal Career Readiness
→ construction contextualization
→ trade-awareness instruction
→ reuse of canonical electrical / HVAC capability where functionally equivalent
→ construction-specific source content only where necessary
→ evidence candidates
→ authorized review where applicable
→ Career transition
→ external trade training / apprenticeship where required

Do not duplicate canonical electrical, HVAC, cybersecurity, Career, Completion, Portfolio, accessibility, evidence, or credential authority.
