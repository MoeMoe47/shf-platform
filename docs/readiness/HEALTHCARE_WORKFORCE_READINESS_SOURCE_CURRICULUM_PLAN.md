## 2. Course Intent

Healthcare Workforce Readiness prepares learners to understand healthcare workplaces, professional expectations, communication, teamwork, safety culture, dignity, privacy, accessibility, digital responsibility, career pathways, evidence, and transition into appropriate next-step training.

The course is a readiness layer.

It is not a substitute for:

- state-approved nurse aide training
- nursing education
- phlebotomy certification training
- EMT or paramedic certification
- clinical instruction
- supervised clinical placement
- employer onboarding
- scope-of-practice authorization
- professional licensure
- healthcare credentialing

Where regulated training is required, SHU readiness prepares the learner to enter that pathway more informed and better prepared.

## 3. Approved Structural Direction

The Pass 1 blueprint proposed:

- 12 units
- 5 lessons per unit
- 60 lessons total

Pass 2 preserves that structure because Healthcare Workforce Readiness requires meaningful healthcare context without duplicating full regulated occupational programs.

The course should remain broad enough to support learners exploring multiple healthcare pathways.

It should not become a hidden STNA, nursing, phlebotomy, EMS, or clinical-skills course.

## 4. Curriculum Classification Model

Every proposed lesson is classified as:

### TRANSFERABLE

The primary learner capability already exists at a cross-industry level and should preferentially reuse Universal Career Readiness concepts.

### CONTEXTUALIZED

The learner capability is transferable, but healthcare examples, terminology, workflow, safety, ethics, or communication context are necessary.

### INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC

The learning objective is meaningfully healthcare-specific and cannot be satisfied by merely changing examples in a universal lesson.

Industry-specific does not mean regulated authority.

## 5. Curriculum Composition Principle

The preferred composition model is:

Universal Career Readiness
→ healthcare contextualization
→ healthcare-specific awareness where genuinely necessary
→ evidence candidates
→ authorized review where applicable
→ Career transition
→ external regulated training where required

Do not recreate universal professional skills merely because the examples occur in a hospital, clinic, laboratory, long-term-care facility, emergency setting, or community-health environment.
