## 2. Course Intent

Logistics & Supply Chain Readiness prepares learners to understand how goods, materials, information, inventory, orders, warehouses, freight, suppliers, customers, documentation, quality, safety, and coordination work together across a supply chain.

The course is a readiness layer.

It is not a substitute for:

- employer-specific warehouse training
- powered industrial truck qualification
- forklift certification
- CDL training
- commercial driver qualification
- hazardous-material endorsement or training
- carrier-specific operating authority
- customs-broker authority
- regulated shipping certification
- union or apprenticeship status

## 5. Classification Model

Each lesson is classified as:

### TRANSFERABLE

The core capability is cross-industry and should preferentially reuse Universal Career Readiness.

### CONTEXTUALIZED

The capability already exists but requires logistics-specific examples, workflows, risks, or terminology.

### INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC

The objective is genuinely logistics / supply-chain specific and cannot be satisfied by changing examples alone.

Industry-specific does not mean regulated or employer-authorized.

## 6. Curriculum Composition Principle

Preferred composition:

Universal Career Readiness
→ logistics contextualization
→ warehouse / inventory / supply-chain process instruction
→ freight coordination context
→ logistics-specific source content only where necessary
→ evidence candidates
→ authorized review where applicable
→ Career transition
→ external employer or regulated training where required

Do not duplicate canonical Transportation, Career, Completion, Portfolio, accessibility, evidence, credential, or organization authority.
