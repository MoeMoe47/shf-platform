## 8. Transportation / Logistics Ownership Boundary

Transportation and Logistics are related but distinct.

Transportation focuses on:

- movement
- vehicles
- fleets
- drivers
- dispatch
- routes
- passenger mobility
- transit
- rail
- aviation
- roadway systems
- transportation incidents

Logistics & Supply Chain Readiness retains primary ownership of:

- warehouse operations
- inventory
- broader logistics processes
- port logistics
- freight documentation
- supply-chain process authority

Transportation may explain freight movement.

It must not become a second Logistics curriculum authority.

## 9. Regulated-Domain Principle

Transportation contains several regulated professional domains.

Readiness instruction may explain:

- roles
- systems
- terminology
- pathways
- documentation
- hazards
- safety culture
- simulated decisions
- escalation
- regulatory awareness

It must not perform or represent external authorization.

## 10. Simulation Principle

Where activities involve regulated operations, use:

- fictional operators
- synthetic vehicles
- synthetic fleet records
- fictional routes
- synthetic dispatch information
- synthetic inspection information
- fictional incidents
- simulated transit conditions
- simulated rail conditions
- fictional airport conditions
- synthetic UAS mission context

Do not represent simulation data as authoritative live transportation operations.
